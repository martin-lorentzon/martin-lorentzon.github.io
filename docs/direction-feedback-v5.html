Plain checkboxes and steppers, so it works with keyboard and screen readers. The panel's rules are simple and written by Martin (each item adds time and files; some items share work). Sending opens e-mail or SMS with the plan filled in, or posts it to a form. No prices unless you want them.

What it carries over

Today's FAQ, made useful: the short call, the quote, regular check-ins; STEP or IGES; a couple of weeks for simpler jobs; SMS or e-mail preferred. The services list is the checklist.

How it works

The map is one component on a normal page; it pans and zooms only when you use it (drag, pinch, + and −), never by hijacking the page scroll. Three zoom levels: city, district, project. "Visa som lista" shows the same content as Q's list, which is also what screen readers get.

What it carries over

Each district is a service, and each marker is a project placed in its district. Verkstaden (the workshop) holds the Blender add-ons. Martin's studio is a marker of its own (About). The river nods to Göta älv without being a real map.

The game-map look must stay generic: our own drawing style and icons, nothing taken from GTA or any other game. Needs enough projects to fill the districts.

An ordinary page with a print stylesheet that lays it out as an A4 sheet; "Download PDF" is the browser's print-to-PDF with that layout, so there's no PDF to maintain. The fact table is the same on every project, so they're easy to compare.

Today's FAQ answers, shown per project: which files were accepted, how long it took, how check-ins worked. The "before a prototype exists" promise shows up as a fact, not a claim. Tools rows quietly advertise the Blender add-ons.

Risk
