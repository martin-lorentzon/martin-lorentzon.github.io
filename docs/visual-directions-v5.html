Company site: visual directions, round 5

Five more practical concepts, built to sit next to H (Provbitarna) and L (Katalogen) in one sleek site. Each one is a tool a buyer can use, not a show. All of them scroll normally: no scroll-jacking anywhere.

These are still pictures of one screen, not working pages. Tag them on the round 5 tag page.

What I took from round 4

  • More of: H's hands-on, practical configurator, L's clear, catalogue-like offer, and J's map look.
  • Less of: scroll-jacking (D), luxury packaging (E), anything too personal (F), showing off (G) and the half-finished render (K).
  • New rule for this round: every concept answers a buyer's real question: what will it look like on my site, what do I need to send, have you done my kind of job, how long does it take.
  • One shared system, so everything combines: Archivo 800 headings (from H), Inter Tight text (from L), JetBrains Mono labels. Colours are L's sand and ink, with H's material colours as the only accents.

O. På din sajt

On your site. See the 3D where it would actually live

Clients don't buy "a 3D viewer"; they buy a better product page. So the site shows one: a realistic web shop page with the 3D viewer in place, its hotspots and its AR button. Pick your field (web shop, industry, medical, education) and the page around the viewer changes to match. A dashed line marks the part Martin delivers.

How it works

Four tabs swap a lightweight page template around the same live viewer: a web shop page, an industrial product sheet, a medical device page, a lesson page. The viewer is real (rotate, tap hotspots, change fabric, open AR). Ordinary page; the mock is one section of it.

What it carries over

Everything today's site says about interactive 3D: configurations, points of interest, moving parts, AR mode, in the browser with no app, fitted into the client's own site. The "fitted into your site" promise becomes visible instead of a sentence.

Combines with

It is the natural page behind L's No. 02 Interaktiv 3D, with H's sample kit as the colour picker inside the viewer.

Risk

The page templates must look like real sites without copying any real brand. Four templates is the limit before it gets heavy.

P. Underlaget

The brief builder. Tick what you need, get a plan

The contact page is a checklist instead of a form. Tick what you need (a film, stills, a 3D viewer, AR, a Blender tool), and a panel beside it builds a plan: about how long it takes, exactly which files to send, and what happens next. You send the plan, not a blank message. Practical for the buyer, and Martin gets a brief he can quote from.

How it works

Plain checkboxes and steppers, so it works with keyboard and screen readers. The panel's rules are simple and written by Martin (each item adds time and files; some items share work). Sending opens e-mail or SMS with the plan filled in, or posts it to a form. No prices unless you want them.

What it carries over

Today's FAQ, made useful: the short call, the quote, regular check-ins; STEP or IGES; a couple of weeks for simpler jobs; SMS or e-mail preferred. The services list is the checklist.

Combines with

The contact page for the whole site. L's catalogue articles each get an "Add to brief" button, like a basket.

Risk

Time estimates must be honest ranges, or they become promises. Keep it to six options.

Q. Arkivet

The archive. Work you can filter like a product range

The work page behaves like a good web shop listing: filter by what was made (film, interactive 3D, tools) and by field (industry, medical, education, consumer), and the grid narrows down. Each card says what was delivered and how long it took. A buyer from a medical company finds medical work in two clicks.

How it works

Two rows of filter buttons that change the address (so a filtered view can be shared: /arbeten?typ=3d&bransch=industri). Works without JavaScript as a plain list. Cards open L-style article pages or S's data sheets.

What it carries over

The three services become the first filter row; the fields today's site names (industry, education, medicine, products) become the second. Blender add-ons sit in the same grid as client work.

Combines with

The "Arbeten" page of the combined site. Every card opens an S data sheet.

Risk

Needs about 12 real projects to feel full; with fewer, hide the field filter until it's useful. The mock's projects are placeholders.

R. Kartan

The map. The whole studio as a game-style city map

J's zoom, made practical. The overview is a flat, top-down city map in the style of open-world game maps: a river through the middle, three districts named after the services, a marker for each project and a "You are here" dot. Hover a marker for a preview, click to open it. It's a sitemap people actually want to use, with a list view one click away.

How it works

The map is one component on a normal page; it pans and zooms only when you use it (drag, pinch, + and −), never by hijacking the page scroll. Three zoom levels: city, district, project. "Visa som lista" shows the same content as Q's list, which is also what screen readers get.

What it carries over

Each district is a service, and each marker is a project placed in its district. Verkstaden (the workshop) holds the Blender add-ons. Martin's studio is a marker of its own (About). The river nods to Göta älv without being a real map.

Combines with

The overview page and the footer of the combined site; also a good 404 page ("you've left the map").

Risk

The game-map look must stay generic: our own drawing style and icons, nothing taken from GTA or any other game. Needs enough projects to fill the districts.

S. Databladet

The data sheet. Every project on one page you can forward

Each case study is a one-page data sheet, like the ones industrial buyers already read: a live 3D view at the top, then a table of facts (the job, what the client sent, what was delivered, how long it took, which tools were used, what it was used for). One button saves it as a PDF, so the person who found the site can forward it to the person who decides.

How it works

An ordinary page with a print stylesheet that lays it out as an A4 sheet; "Download PDF" is the browser's print-to-PDF with that layout, so there's no PDF to maintain. The fact table is the same on every project, so they're easy to compare.

What it carries over

Today's FAQ answers, shown per project: which files were accepted, how long it took, how check-ins worked. The "before a prototype exists" promise shows up as a fact, not a claim. Tools rows quietly advertise the Blender add-ons.

Combines with

Every project page in the combined site. It's L's catalogue spec, scaled up to a whole case.

Risk

Dry if the 3D view and images aren't strong. Client names and numbers need permission; the mock's project is an example.

Recommendation

Build the combination above. Of the five new ones, O (På din sajt) is the strongest: it answers the buyer's first question, "what would this look like for us?", and it makes interactive 3D, the most valuable service, concrete.

P and S are the workhorses (cheap to build, useful every day), Q becomes worth it once there are about 12 projects, and R is the one people will remember, so keep it for the overview and the 404 page rather than the front door.
